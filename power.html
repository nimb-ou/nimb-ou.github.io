Statistical Reconciliations: Why 22.2% vs 29.9%. The declared naive baseline in the codebase was same_period_last_week, against which XGBoost achieves a 29.9% MAE improvement (MAE 27.20 vs 38.80 £/MWh). However, the toughest persistence baseline is same_period_two_days_ago (MAE 34.95 £/MWh), yielding an honest 22.2% MAE improvement. Furthermore, the weather ablation experiment (re-running the entire 4.5-year walk-forward without weather features) yields MAE 30.22 £/MWh, also matching the 22.1% bracket.

1. Mathematical Validation & Statistical Significance

Time-series model comparisons cannot rely on plain paired t-tests due to serial autocorrelation. We evaluate model superiority using the rigorous Diebold-Mariano test:

Diebold-Mariano Loss Differential Hypothesis Test

For loss differential \(d_t = |e_{t, \text{naive}}| - |e_{t, \text{XGB}}|\), the test statistic accounts for autocorrelation:

\[ DM = \frac{\bar{d}}{\sqrt{\hat{\sigma}_d^2 / N}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \]

Empirical evaluation over 76,536 paired periods: \(DM = -28.39\) (\(p = 2.82 \times 10^{-177}\)), conclusively rejecting the null hypothesis of equal forecast accuracy in favor of XGBoost.

Conformal Prediction Interval Calibration

Raw quantile loss (\(\text{P10}\)–\(\text{P90}\)) optimizes the asymmetric pinball loss:

\[ \mathcal{L}_q(y, \hat{y}) = \max(q(y - \hat{y}), (1 - q)(\hat{y} - y)) \]

Because uncalibrated pinball intervals achieve only 50.2% empirical coverage during high-volatility regimes, we wrap predictions in a 60-day rolling conformal calibrator that restores empirical coverage to 75.6% on a nominal 80% interval.
