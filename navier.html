1. Mathematical Theory & Formal Theorems

By the Beale-Kato-Majda (BKM) criterion, a smooth classical solution \(u \in C([0, T^*); H^s)\) ceases to exist at \(T^*\) if and only if accumulated vorticity diverges:

Beale-Kato-Majda Blowup Criterion

\[ \int_0^{T^*} \|\omega(\cdot, t)\|_{L^\infty} \, dt = \infty \]

A central mathematical challenge in 3D fluid singularities is constructing blowup candidates that maintain finite initial kinetic energy. In classical isotropic self-similar profiles (\(\ell_r \sim \ell_z \sim \tau^{1/2}\)), the kinetic energy diverges. Our formalization analyzes an anisotropic contraction where the radial scale \(\ell_r \sim \tau^{1/2}\) contracts faster than the axial scale \(\ell_z \sim \tau^{1/2-h}\):

\[ E_{\text{core}}(t) = \int_{\text{core}} |u|^2 \, dx \sim \ell_r^2 \cdot \ell_z \cdot \|u\|_{L^\infty}^2 \sim \tau^1 \cdot \tau^{1/2-h} \cdot \tau^{-1-2h} = \tau^{1/2-3h} \xrightarrow[\tau \to 0]{} 0 \]

For \(0 < h < 1/6\), core kinetic energy decays strictly to zero as the vortex contracts into an infinitely thin rotating needle with bounded global energy.
