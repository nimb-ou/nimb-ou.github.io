1. Foundational Theory & Mathematics

The 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations describe momentum balance in viscous Newtonian fluids:

\partial_t u + (u \cdot \nabla)u = -\nabla p + \nu \Delta u + f, \quad \nabla \cdot u = 0

Leray Projector & Sobolev Spaces

Projecting onto divergence-free vector fields eliminates the non-local pressure gradient:

\mathbb{P}(v) = v - \nabla \Delta^{-1}(\nabla \cdot v) \implies \partial_t u + \mathbb{P}((u \cdot \nabla)u) = \nu \Delta u

By the Beale-Kato-Majda (BKM) criterion, a smooth solution \(u \in C([0, T); H^s)\) ceases to exist at \(T^*\) if and only if the accumulated vorticity diverges:

\int_0^{T^*} \|\omega(\cdot, t)\|_{L^\infty} \, dt = \infty, \quad \text{where } \omega = \nabla \times u

The Anisotropic Blowup Candidate

A major open challenge is constructing finite-time singularities that retain finite initial energy. If the core contracts isotropically as \(\tau^{1/2}\), the kinetic energy diverges. Our mathematical formalization and simulation demonstrate an anisotropic contraction where the radial scale \(\ell_r \sim \tau^{1/2}\) contracts faster than the axial scale \(\ell_z \sim \tau^{1/2-h}\), causing the vortex to form an infinitely thin rotating needle while core kinetic energy decays to zero:

E_{\text{core}}(t) = \int_{\text{core}} |u|^2 \, dx \sim \ell_r^2 \, \ell_z \cdot \|u\|_{L^\infty}^2 \sim \tau^1 \cdot \tau^{1/2-h} \cdot \tau^{-1-2h} = \tau^{1/2-3h} \xrightarrow[\tau \to 0]{} 0 \quad (\text{for } 0 < h < 1/6)

4. Failures, Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

The Isotropic Divergence Trap

Initial numerical implementations enforced equal scaling \(\ell_r = \ell_z = \tau^{1/2}\). This produced unphysical energy divergence at \(\tau = 10^{-3}\), violating Ladyzhenskaya bounds. Introducing the small perturbation \(h = 0.005\) in the axial dimension resolved the divergence and preserved bounded energy.

Formal Prover Strictness

Translating textbook PDE steps into Lean 4 required proving uniform convergence on boundary integrals that informal literature glosses over as "standard". Mathlib's Bochner integration forced explicit handling of support compactification.
