1. Mathematical Foundations & Space Vector Geometry

Three-phase balanced voltages \((v_a, v_b, v_c)\) project onto a complex orthogonal stationary plane \((\alpha, \beta)\) via the Clarke transformation:

Clarke Stationary Reference Frame

\[ \begin{bmatrix} v_\alpha \\ v_\beta \end{bmatrix} = \frac{2}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & -\frac{1}{2} \\ 0 & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_a \\ v_b \\ v_c \end{bmatrix} \]

In pure Sinusoidal PWM (SPWM), the maximum modulation index without overmodulation is \(m_a = 1.0\), yielding fundamental line-to-line RMS voltage \(V_{LL} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{2}} V_{dc} \approx 0.612 V_{dc}\). Space Vector PWM adds an optimal zero-sequence triplen harmonic, extending the linear modulation ceiling by:

\[ m_{a, \max} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 1.155 \quad (+15.5\% \text{ DC-Bus Utilization}) \]

Discontinuous PWM1 (DPWM1) clamps each inverter phase leg to the positive or negative DC rail for 60° of each fundamental cycle (120° total per cycle). Because no switching occurs during the clamped interval, total inverter switching cycles drop by exactly one-third: \[ \Delta P_{\text{sw}} = \frac{120^\circ}{360^\circ} = 33.33\% \]
