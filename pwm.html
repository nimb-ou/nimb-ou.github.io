1. Foundational Theory & Mathematics

Modulation algorithms map stationary three-phase reference voltages into discrete switching states across inverter legs.

Space Vector Clarke Transformation

Any balanced three-phase voltage set \((v_a, v_b, v_c)\) projects onto a two-dimensional orthogonal complex plane \((\alpha, \beta)\) via the Clarke transform:

\begin{bmatrix} v_\alpha \\ v_\beta \end{bmatrix} = \frac{2}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & -\frac{1}{2} \\ 0 & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} v_a \\ v_b \\ v_c \end{bmatrix}

A 2-level inverter has 8 discrete switching states: 6 active vectors dividing the plane into \(60^\circ\) sectors, and 2 zero vectors \(\mathbf{V}_0(000)\) and \(\mathbf{V}_7(111)\).

The 15.5% Voltage Utilization Boost

In pure Sinusoidal PWM (SPWM), the maximum modulation index without overmodulation is \(m_a = 1.0\), yielding a fundamental line-to-line RMS voltage of \(V_{LL} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{2}} V_{dc} \approx 0.612 V_{dc}\).

By injecting optimal zero-sequence voltages \(v_{zs}(t) = -\frac{1}{2}\big(\max(v_a, v_b, v_c) + \min(v_a, v_b, v_c)\big)\) (as in SVPWM and THIPWM), the reference wave peaks are flattened. This expands the linear modulation boundary by \(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \approx 1.155\) (a 15.5% increase in available DC-bus voltage), allowing electric motors to deliver higher torque at high speeds without entering distortive overmodulation.

4. Failures, Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

Dead-Time Waveform Distortion

In physical silicon, turning off an IGBT/MOSFET takes finite time. To prevent shoot-through short circuits across the DC bus, microcontrollers insert a 1.2μs dead-time. During this blanking period, terminal voltage is dictated by motor phase current direction rather than gating signals. This introduces severe 5th and 7th low-order harmonic spikes. Solution: PyPWM incorporates feedforward voltage compensation pulses in the timer ISR to neutralize dead-time voltage drops.

DPWM1 33% Loss Reduction vs Ripple

DPWM1 clamps each phase leg to the DC rail for \(60^\circ\) each fundamental cycle, completely halting switching events during the phase's peak current interval. This cuts switching heat losses by 33%. The trade-off is higher low-frequency harmonic current ripple at low modulation indices (\(m_a < 0.6\)). Our firmware dynamically switches from SVPWM at low speeds to DPWM1 at high speeds to maximize overall powertrain efficiency.
