1. Foundational Theory & Mathematics

Debt networks form directed weighted graphs \(G = (V, E)\) whose edges can be compressed via conservation of cash flow.

Conservation of Flow & Net Balances

Let \(V = \{1, 2, \dots, N\}\) denote the set of participants. For each participant \(i\), their net balance \(\Delta_i\) is the difference between total funds paid into the group and total funds consumed:

$$\Delta_i = \sum_{j=1}^N \text{PaidTo}(i, j) - \sum_{j=1}^N \text{BorrowedFrom}(i, j)$$

By definition of closed transactions, the system is strictly zero-sum:

$$\sum_{i=1}^N \Delta_i = 0$$

The Greedy Settlement Heuristic

Finding the absolute global minimum number of transfers is reducible from the NP-complete Subset Sum problem (finding disjoint subsets of participants whose balances sum exactly to zero).

The greedy algorithm provides a fast, deterministic polynomial-time approximation \(\mathcal{O}(N \log N)\):

  1. Identify maximum debtor \(D = \arg\min_i \Delta_i\) and maximum creditor \(C = \arg\max_i \Delta_i\).
  2. Settle amount \(M = \min(-\Delta_D, \Delta_C)\) from \(D\) to \(C\).
  3. Update balances: \(\Delta_D \leftarrow \Delta_D + M\) and \(\Delta_C \leftarrow \Delta_C - M\).
  4. Repeat until all balances equal zero. At least one participant's balance reaches zero in each iteration, guaranteeing termination in at most \(N-1\) steps.

4. Failures, Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

The Trust-Boundary Paradox

Pure mathematical debt simplification can yield socially uncomfortable settlements: e.g. Alice paying Charlie (whom she does not know) instead of Bob (her friend). Solution: In production systems, debt graphs must support "trust clusters" or sub-group bounding to prevent cross-boundary payments between unlinked users.

Floating-Point Odd-Cent Leakage

Splitting a ₹1,000 bill across 3 people yields ₹333.3333... If stored as naive floats, \(\sum \Delta_i = -0.01\), violating the fundamental zero-sum conservation invariant and causing infinite loops in greedy heaps. Fix: All calculations operate strictly in integer cents/paise with remainder allocation assigned to the payer.
