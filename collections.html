1. Foundational Theory & Mathematics

Collections operations operate on stochastic transition dynamics and asymmetric payoff matrices.

Markov Delinquency State Transition

At monthly billing cycles \(t\), the portfolio transitions across discrete states \(S = \{C, \text{SMA}_0, \text{SMA}_1, \text{SMA}_2, B_1, B_2, B_3, \text{NPA}\}\) governed by stochastic transition matrix \(P\):

P_{ij} = \mathbb{P}(S_{t+1} = j \mid S_t = i, \, \mathbf{x}, \, a)

where \(\mathbf{x}\) is the borrower feature vector (salary cadence, historical bounce rate, bureau inquiries) and \(a \in \mathcal{A}\) is the assigned treatment intervention. The objective is minimizing expected credit cost:

\min_{\pi} \sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{E}\Big[ \text{Cost}(S_t) + C_{\text{action}}(a_t) \Big] \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \text{ContactCeiling}(a) \le \text{Limit}_{\text{RBI}}

Asymmetric Cost Matrix & Self-Cure Cannibalization

Calling a borrower who would have cured on their own ("Self-Cure") incurs unnecessary operational expense and agency fatigue, while failing to visit a high-balance borrower who rolls into NPA costs the bank the entire principal write-off.

4. Failures, Trade-offs & Lessons Learned

The High Self-Cure Trap

In early testing, calling all D+1 bounced accounts triggered significant customer complaints and high vendor costs. Analysis proved 42% of low-risk borrowers self-cure within 5 days of salary credit. Suppressing outbound calls for high self-cure accounts cut operational costs by 28% with zero increase in B2 roll rates.

Strict Compliance Lift

Enforcing hard limits on agent calling windows and frequency caps actually increased right-party contact (RPC) rates by 14%: agents focused on optimal response windows rather than wasting attempts during unreachable working hours.
